Phase 1 · Time-Critical

Window-lit shots. Shoot first.

~ 30 minutes · 2 shots · Couch → Bedroom doorway · Natural light

Shot 01 Window Light 20 sec Living Room 0:00 – 0:20

OPEN — Journal on the couch.

Setup: Sit on the couch. Journal in your lap. Window to your side, soft fading window light hitting the page. a7C on tripod — back the tripod to the far wall, 85mm needs 8–10 ft for a natural couch shot. Kill every other light in the living room.

Action: Pen lands on the page. Write the word "Reset" slowly. Hold 3 seconds after the word lands.

85mm note: The compression will flatten the background so the window light becomes the whole world behind you. Keep focus manually on the journal page. At this distance, shallow DOF means the page is sharp and the room falls off beautifully.
Continuity note: Same couch, same window, same journal as STILL. The visual rhyme connects the two films — different energy, same anchor.

Shot 04 Window + Accent 30 sec Living Room → Bedroom 1:10 – 1:40

THE ROOM — Two cuts. Solo. No gimbal.

Cut A — Living room tripod. Camera as wide as you can get it (use your phone propped on a surface if 85mm is too tight). You're on the couch. Stand up slowly. Hold 2 seconds standing. Don't walk out of frame — just stand. Let the light catch you. End there.

Cut B — Bedroom tripod, low angle, pointed at the doorway. Lock off. Walk through the door from the hallway into frame, slow heel-to-toe. Sit down on the bed. Camera never moves. You do. Shoot while there's still enough ambient light to see the room — once it's pitch black this shot dies.

Solo fix: The edit connects these two cuts and the audience feels the journey — same as a gimbal walk, just two locked-off frames instead of one moving one. No one in the room required.
Narrative note: The journey is implied — couch (public, where you still have light) to bedroom (private, where you give up). The viewer travels with you without needing to see the hallway.

Phase 2 · After Dark

Accent + softbox. 7 shots. Same setup reused.

~ 2–3 hours · No time pressure · Set it once, don't touch it

85mm room setup for all Phase 2 shots: Tripod at the far wall or in the doorway. Mark your tripod position once and don't move it between shots — just adjust distance for tight vs. wide. At f/2.0 you need ~8–10 ft for chest-up, ~6–7 ft for tight face. The accent rim on your shoulder and ear will glow harder at 85mm than any wider lens. This is the right tool.
Shot 02 Accent + Softbox 25 sec 0:20 – 0:45

PORTRAIT — Sitting in the warm rim.

Setup: Sit on the bed leaning back. Accent light behind, pointed up at the wall. Softbox to your side, dim or bounced. Camera 8–10 feet away, slightly below eye level.

Action: Face the camera. Don't perform. Half your face in soft fill, warm rim from the accent on your shoulder and hair. Hold 8–10 seconds before any movement.

85mm note: At 8–10 ft, f/2.0, the background falls completely off into warm bokeh. The accent rim will separate your shoulder from the dark in a way a 35mm can't touch.

THIRST — Tight on the face.

Shot 05 Accent Reflected 35 sec 1:40 – 2:15

REFLECTION — Watching yourself.

Setup: Use a closed laptop, your room window after dark, or a dresser mirror. Accent light reflects in the surface as a soft warm glow. Camera behind you, slightly off-center, capturing your face in the reflection without seeing itself.

Action: Sit or stand looking at the surface. Not posing. Just watching. The conversation you have with yourself when no one else is in the room.

85mm note: Focus manually on the reflection surface — the 85mm minimum focus distance is ~2.8 ft so you have room. The compression makes the reflection feel like a second world. The accent glow in the surface doubles as a motivated light source behind your reflected face.

Shot 06 Screen + Accent 35 sec 2:15 – 2:50

EMAIL — Between two lights. Cold and warm.

Setup: Sit on the bed with the laptop in front of you. Laptop screen (cool blue) lights your face from below. Accent (warm orange) glows behind. Kill the softbox for this one. Just the two motivated sources.

Action: Reading the screen. Let your face react or don't. The contrast between cool blue and warm orange is the entire shot.

85mm note: At 8–9 ft the laptop screen will light your face without the camera being in the reflection. The 85mm keeps you isolated from the dark room — all the audience sees is the two competing colors on your face.

Shot 07 Accent + Softbox Pivot · 35 sec 2:50 – 3:25

STAYING — Direct to camera. The film's center.

Setup: Same as Shot 02. Sit on the bed. Accent rim behind. Softbox feathered. Most important shot of the film.

Action: Look straight into the lens. Two-second silence first. Then deliver the line live — not voiceover. Hold 4 seconds after the line lands.

85mm note: The 85mm at 8 ft makes this a true connection shot — the compression removes the room and leaves only you and whoever is watching. The eye contact at this focal length is intentional and unavoidable. Deliver the line to the glass, not around it.

GALATIANS — Bible by the accent light.

Camera + Audio + Phone

Camera Mode Manual. Full control over exposure. No auto.
Lens 85mm f/2.0. Portrait lens. Set once per shot, mark your tripod position.
ISO 1600–3200. This is a dark film. Embrace the higher ISO — the a7C handles it fine.
Aperture f/2.0 for portraits and face shots. f/2.8 for the Bible page (more depth).
Shutter 1/50 at 24fps. The 180° shutter rule. Cinematic motion blur.
White Balance ~3200K. Honor the warm accent light. Don't auto-correct it away.
Audio Voiceover recorded separately — indoors, in a closet or under blankets to kill reverb. Don't try to capture clean audio during the shoot.
Phone Do Not Disturb. Brightness all the way down. Face-down when not actively filming so it can't bleed light into a shot.
